## Finding

The I Ching and related symbolic systems are historically important combinatorial change texts. Claims that they encode advanced lost technology should not be treated as evidence. The useful NexusNet transfer is a formal change calculus: finite symbols, transitions, interpretations, uncertainty, and reflective decision checkpoints.

## NexusNet Assimilation Target

Create an archaic-symbolic inspiration lane that converts historical change systems into modern, testable design exercises: enumerate states, define transformations, map uncertainty, generate interpretations, and compare them with evidence.

## Proposed NexusNet Components

- `ChangeStateSymbol`: finite state marker for system mood, risk, uncertainty, or development phase.
- `TransitionOracle`: deterministic or stochastic transition rule with no supernatural claim.
- `InterpretationLayer`: maps symbolic transitions to practical questions and review prompts.
- `EvidenceCounterweight`: forces every symbolic suggestion to be checked against current traces and sources.
- `MythicInspirationBoundary`: labels historical/mythic material as inspiration only.

## Promotion Gates

- Never promote mythic or esoteric claims as factual evidence without independent support.
- Translate symbolic systems into testable state machines or prompts.
- Require modern source evidence before any implementation decision.
- Keep the lane opt-in and visibly separated from scientific/engineering specs.
- Avoid religious or cultural appropriation in product language.

## Risks

- Symbolic systems can encourage pattern overfitting.
- Operators may confuse inspiration with proof.
- Cultural material requires respect, context, and careful sourcing.
