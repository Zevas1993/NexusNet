# Lullian Combinatorial Search Engine Spec

Status: P2 inspiration target. Historical combinatorics only; not evidence of lost technology.

## Finding

Ramon Llull's Ars used figures, alphabets, questions, and combinations to generate arguments. Its useful NexusNet transfer is controlled combinatorial ideation: define primitives, combine them systematically, score them, reject contradictions, and preserve surprising viable combinations.

## NexusNet Assimilation Target

Create a Lullian search engine for NexusNet research, architecture, and self-improvement proposals. The engine should recombine approved primitives from the assimilation packet and generate candidate designs with explicit assumptions and contradiction checks.

## Proposed NexusNet Components

- `PrimitiveAlphabet`: approved concepts such as memory, router, replay, sandbox, policy, cortex, growth, and provenance.
- `CombinationFigure`: constrained combinatorial template for joining primitives.
- `QuestionWheel`: prompts such as why, how, by what authority, under what evidence, and with what failure mode.
- `ContradictionFilter`: rejects combinations that violate policy, physics, evidence, or product boundaries.
- `SteppingStoneCandidate`: surprising combination preserved for later review.

## Promotion Gates

- Only combine primitives with known source status.
- Require contradiction and safety filtering before human review.
- Preserve weird but plausible stepping stones separately from implementation plans.
- Do not let combinatorial novelty override evidence.
- Record which primitive sources produced each candidate.

## Risks

- Combinatorial systems can create enormous low-value search spaces.
- The engine may produce poetic but unbuildable ideas.
- Historical analogy should remain inspiration, not authority.
